Persian AI in 2026: The Best Local Models for Farsi Speakers

Published: August 9, 2026 · Updated: October 7, 2026 — Persian (Farsi) has a real ecosystem of dedicated models and benchmarks. But which open models actually perform well? Here's what the two most relevant 2025 benchmarks found — one on raw Persian NLP tasks and one on Persian language and culture — plus the Persian-specific models worth knowing.

🌍 Quick Takeaways

  • Gemma 2 is the strongest open model for Persian overall — it led a 2025 benchmark across QA, reasoning, reading, sentiment, and generation
  • GLM-4 and Qwen2.5 are the next-best multilingual bases, with Qwen2.5 best at zero-shot logical reasoning
  • Newer generation: Gemma 3 (Gemma 2's successor) and Aya Expanse 32B have verified Persian results via the MELAC cultural benchmark
  • Qwen2 is the surprise specialist at Persian named-entity recognition (0.82 F1, best in the study)
  • Persian-specific models exist and are useful: PersianMind, Maral, PersianLLaMA, Dorna, Hormoz
  • Popular ≠ best: Llama 3.x scored near the bottom on Persian tasks in the same benchmark

The Persian AI Landscape in 2026

Persian is one of the best-served "underdog" languages in open AI: a community of researchers and builders has produced dedicated models, datasets, and benchmarks. The open-source Persian-specific models include PersianMind, Maral, and PersianLLaMA (plus the newer Dorna, Dorna2, and Hormoz-8B), and the Awesome-Persian-LLM repository curates the whole ecosystem — papers, datasets, models, tools, and evaluation benchmarks.

But the more important question in 2026 is which general open-weight models actually handle Persian well. The answer, per two independent 2025 benchmarks, is more specific than the usual "Qwen, Llama, and Gemma" shorthand — and it upends one common assumption.

What the 2025 NLP Benchmark Actually Found

A peer-reviewed study (Cherakhloo et al., 2025 — arXiv:2510.12807) evaluated 11 open models in zero-shot and few-shot settings across Persian NLI, reading comprehension, sentiment, named-entity recognition, question answering (PersianQA, Persian-SQuAD), reasoning, summarization, and generation — using established datasets such as ParsiNLU, ArmanEmo, and ArmanNER. Here is the overall few-shot ranking (averaged across NLI, QA, reasoning, generation, and basic tasks):

Two findings stand out. First, Gemma 2 is the clear leader — it wins or ties nearly every task and is especially strong on Persian reasoning and QA. Second, Llama 3.x performs poorly on Persian despite its popularity, so "just grab a Llama" is the wrong default for Farsi work.

One consistent weakness: named-entity recognition. Most models collapse to near-zero on zero-shot NER, and only Qwen2 (0.82) handles it well few-shot — a reminder to pick models per task, not per brand.

Newer Models (2025–2026): Gemma 3 and Aya Expanse

The benchmark above tested the 2024 generation. Since then, two families have the clearest verified Persian evidence, via the MELAC benchmark — a 2025 study that evaluated 41 models on 19 Persian datasets spanning Iranian law, Persian grammar, idioms, literature, and university entrance exams (using private test sets to reduce contamination).

  • Gemma 3 (Google, 2025) — the direct successor to the benchmark-leading Gemma 2, and included in MELAC's recent-model set. It's the natural upgrade path if you liked Gemma 2's Persian results.
  • Aya Expanse 32B (Cohere For AI) — the standout on cultural alignment. It posted strong MELAC scores (up to ~94 on several categories) even though it was mid-tier on the raw NLP benchmark above. When cultural and contextual accuracy matters more than raw task scores, Aya Expanse is worth testing.

The other 2025-generation multilingual releases — Qwen3, Llama 4, and GPT-OSS — are strong candidates on paper, but they have thinner verified Persian-specific numbers in the public benchmarks I reviewed as of this update. So the safe, evidence-backed recommendations remain Gemma 2 → GLM-4 → Qwen2.5 for general work, with Gemma 3 and Aya Expanse 32B as the newer picks to test.

The pragmatic default in 2026: Gemma 2 for general Persian work (Gemma 3 as the upgrade), Qwen2.5 as the runner-up, Qwen2 when you need entity extraction, and Aya Expanse 32B when cultural accuracy is the priority. Reach for a Persian-specific model when you want a specialist optimized for the language, and fine-tune or glossary-lock when your task has specific terminology.

Running It Locally

Persian AI fits the same hardware math as everything else: roughly 0.5GB of memory per billion parameters for quantized models. A 7–9B quantized Persian-capable model — Gemma 2, GLM-4, or Qwen2.5 — runs on a modern 8–16GB laptop, fully offline. Aya Expanse 32B needs more headroom (quantized, on a 24GB+ machine). For sizing, see How Much RAM Do You Need to Run Local AI? and Top 10 GGUF Models Ranked by RAM.

Three Techniques That Actually Improve Persian AI

  1. Fine-tune on clean Persian data. A small, curated Persian dataset adapted onto a strong multilingual base (Gemma 2/3, GLM-4, or Qwen2.5) outperforms a generic model every time. LoRA makes this cheap — see Fine-Tuning a Local LLM: LoRA for Beginners.
  2. Lock the glossary. For legal, medical, or government terminology, a glossary-constrained pipeline keeps translation consistent — the single highest-leverage technique in regional languages. See Building a Multilingual Translation Pipeline with Local LLMs.
  3. Evaluate in Persian. Measure with Persian benchmarks (ParsiNLU, Persian MMLU, ArmanEmo, MELAC, FaMTEB), not English ones. What gets measured gets improved.

Why Local Matters for Persian Speakers

Cloud AI may have larger Persian capacity, but it comes with strings: subscriptions, data leaving the device, and — for speakers in restricted environments — availability problems. Local Persian AI is free, private, unlimited, and offline. For institutional work — legal, medical, government — it's often the only acceptable option, and it puts the capability in the hands of the speakers themselves rather than the platforms that under-serve them.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Are there Persian-specific LLMs?

Yes. Open-source Persian-specific models include PersianMind, Maral, PersianLLaMA, and Llama 2 7B Persian — plus the newer Dorna, Dorna2, and Hormoz-8B — and the Awesome-Persian-LLM repository curates the ecosystem. Multilingual bases like Gemma 2, Gemma 3, GLM-4, and Qwen2.5 also perform well in Persian.

Which model is best for Persian AI in 2026?

On a 2025 benchmark of 11 open models across Persian tasks (arXiv:2510.12807), Gemma 2 led overall, followed by GLM-4 and Qwen2.5. For the newer generation, Gemma 3 (Gemma 2's successor) and Aya Expanse 32B have verified Persian results via the MELAC cultural benchmark. Qwen2 was strongest at Persian named-entity recognition.

Can I run a Persian AI model on a laptop?

Yes. 7–9B quantized models such as Gemma 2 and Qwen2.5 run on 8–16GB RAM laptops, fully offline and private. Persian-specific small models like Maral also fit. Aya Expanse 32B needs more RAM (24GB+ machine recommended).

How do I improve Persian AI quality locally?

Fine-tune a multilingual base on clean Persian data, use glossary-locked translation for terminology, and evaluate on Persian-specific benchmarks rather than English ones. Small curated datasets go a long way.

Is Persian AI better in the cloud?

Cloud APIs may have larger Persian capacity, but local models are private, free, unlimited, and offline. For sensitive or institutional Persian work — legal, medical, government — local deployment is often the only acceptable option.
